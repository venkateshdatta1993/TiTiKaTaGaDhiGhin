What good editing is

Good editing is invisible. Every cut happens for a reason (a beat, a movement, a feeling), so the viewer simply feels the dance.

Learn from the masters

  • Book In the Blink of an Eye, Walter Murch A short, beautiful book on why a cut works. His "Rule of Six" puts emotion first, then story, then rhythm.
  • YouTube Every Frame a Painting Watch the Jackie Chan episode on filming movement: let the action play in a wide shot, and cut on the hit.

Kathak editing patterns

01 · Hook

Hook in the first 1–2 seconds

Open on a chakkar mid-spin or the sound of ghungroo before anything else, so viewers stop scrolling.

02 · Rhythm

Cut on the beat

Time your cuts to the tabla bols, and land a cut exactly on the final "dha" of a tihai.

03 · Flow

Cut on motion

Cut in the middle of a spin, so the spin started in one shot finishes in the next. The movement carries the viewer across the cut.

04 · Variety

Mix shot sizes

Use wide shots for full phrases, and close-ups for footwork, hastas (hands) and abhinaya (expression).

05 · Breath

Don't cut mid-phrase

Let complete movements breathe. A tukda shown whole is more powerful than a chopped-up one.

06 · Drama

Speed-ramp into slow-mo

Ramp into slow motion on spins and finishing poses, then snap back to normal speed on the beat.

Shooting

DJI Osmo Pocket 4

  • 4K / 240 fps slow-motion: for chakkars, ghungroo close-ups and the moment of a finishing pose.
  • ActiveTrack 7.0: draw a box around yourself on the screen and the camera follows you across the floor.
  • Spin Shot: the camera rotates as you film. It's made for chakkars.
  • Dynamic Framing: re-frames automatically to keep you well composed.
  • 10-bit D-Log: a flat-looking recording that keeps more colour detail, so you can grade warm, rich tones later.
DJI feature tutorials on YouTube ↗

iPhone 17 Pro Max

Free "Today at Apple" video sessions run in UK Apple Stores, and you can book one online.

Book a Today at Apple session ↗
  • Long-press the screen to lock focus and exposure (AE/AF Lock), so the picture doesn't pulse as you move.
  • Shoot in good light: face a window, or use soft light from the front and side.
  • Use the main 1× lens in dim spaces. It's the sharpest and handles low light best.
  • Use a tripod for wide, fixed shots of full phrases.

The two-camera setup

Camera A

iPhone on a tripod

The wide master shot, running the whole time, with your full body in frame.

Camera B

Pocket 4 handheld

Moving close-ups: feet, hands, face and spins.

In the edit: start on the wide shot. Cut to a close-up for detail on a bol, and cut back to the wide shot for the tihai. Clap once on camera at the start so you can line up both clips' audio.
