What good editing is

Good editing is invisible. Every cut happens for a reason (a beat, a movement, a feeling), so the viewer simply feels the dance.

Learn from the masters

  • Book In the Blink of an Eye, Walter Murch A short, beautiful book on why a cut works. His "Rule of Six" puts emotion first, then story, then rhythm.
  • YouTube Every Frame a Painting Watch the Jackie Chan episode on filming movement: let the action play in a wide shot, and cut on the hit.
    Find the Jackie Chan episode ↗
  • Course Mike Tyus: Filming Dance Videos A dancer-filmmaker's masterclass on framing, camera movement and rhythm.

Kathak editing patterns

01 · Hook

Hook in the first 1–2 seconds

Open on a chakkar mid-spin or the sound of ghungroo before anything else, so viewers stop scrolling.

02 · Rhythm

Cut on the beat

Time your cuts to the tabla bols, and land a cut exactly on the final "dha" of a tihai.

03 · Flow

Cut on motion

Cut in the middle of a spin, so the spin started in one shot finishes in the next. The movement carries the viewer across the cut.

04 · Variety

Mix shot sizes

Use wide shots for full phrases, and close-ups for footwork, hastas (hands) and abhinaya (expression).

05 · Breath

Don't cut mid-phrase

Let complete movements breathe. A tukda shown whole is more powerful than a chopped-up one.

06 · Drama

Speed-ramp into slow-mo

Ramp into slow motion on spins and finishing poses, then snap back to normal speed on the beat.

Shooting

DJI Osmo Pocket 4

  • 4K / 240 fps slow-motion: for chakkars, ghungroo close-ups and the moment of a finishing pose.
  • ActiveTrack 7.0: draw a box around yourself on the screen and the camera follows you across the floor.
  • Spin Shot: the camera rotates as you film. It's made for chakkars.
  • Dynamic Framing: re-frames automatically to keep you well composed.
  • 10-bit D-Log: a flat-looking recording that keeps more colour detail, so you can grade warm, rich tones later.

iPhone 17 Pro Max

  • Long-press the screen to lock focus and exposure (AE/AF Lock), so the picture doesn't pulse as you move.
  • Shoot in good light: face a window, or use soft light from the front and side.
  • Use the main 1× lens in dim spaces. It's the sharpest and handles low light best.
  • Use a tripod for wide, fixed shots of full phrases.

The two-camera setup

Camera A

iPhone on a tripod

The wide master shot, running the whole time, with your full body in frame.

Camera B

Pocket 4 handheld

Moving close-ups: feet, hands, face and spins.

In the edit: start on the wide shot. Cut to a close-up for detail on a bol, and cut back to the wide shot for the tihai. Clap once on camera at the start so you can line up both clips' audio.
4

Cutting to music

Turning separate clips into one beautiful, on-beat video is mostly planned before you film. The edit then just puts the pieces together.

Before you film

  1. Film to the music This is the biggest secret. Play the actual song out loud during every take, in every outfit and location. You dance the same choreography at the same moment of the song each time, so every clip lines up and you can cut from garden to studio mid-phrase.
  2. Same phrase, many angles Dance the same 15 seconds once wide, once close on the feet, once on the face and hands, and once from a low angle. Then you have a choice of shots at every beat.
  3. Shoot B-roll Short detail shots with no full dance: ghungroo being tied, a mudra, the ghera flaring, feet on the floor, light through a window. They set the mood and cover imperfect moments.
  4. Keep it consistent Use the same frame rate, the same camera height and similar light across takes, so clips filmed at different times feel like one film.

In the edit

  • Mark the beats: CapCut's Beats / Auto beat marks them for you; in Edits, add beat markers by hand.
  • Cut on strong beats, especially the sam and the final dha of a tihai.
  • Cut during movement: a spin starts in one clip and finishes in the next, so the cut disappears.

Transitions that hide the cut

Signature

Chakkar transition

Spin in outfit A, cut mid-spin, and finish the spin in outfit B or a new place. This is the classic dance-reel move.

Cover

Hand or dupatta wipe

Sweep a hand or dupatta across the lens. The next clip starts with it sweeping away.

Energy

Whip pan

Move the camera fast at the end of one clip and the start of the next, in the same direction.

Elegant

Match cut

End on a pose, then begin the next clip in the same pose somewhere else.

Give the reel a shape

Hook0–2 sec: the most striking moment
BuildRising energy, mixed shots
PeakFastest footwork or a tihai
ResolveA held final pose
Slow motion is a spice, not the meal: save it for a spin or the final pose, so it still feels special.

Colour & light
Step 1

Colour correction

Fix the footage: correct brightness and accurate colours.

Step 2

Colour grading

Give it a look: warm and golden, moody, or soft. Always correct first, then grade.

The order to adjust

Use the Adjust panel in Edits or CapCut, in this order:

  1. Exposure / brightness Her face should be clearly lit. Lower highlights to bring back a bright window; raise shadows to see into dark corners.
  2. White balance (temperature / tint) Make whites look white. Indoor bulbs make footage orange; shade makes it blue.
  3. Contrast Add a little for depth. Too much makes skin harsh.
  4. Saturation / vibrance Go gently; vibrance is kinder to skin. Costumes and gold jewellery pop easily.
  5. Then add a look A filter or LUT, with its strength lowered to 30–60%.

Making separate clips match

  • Correct one "hero" clip until it looks right, then copy those settings to the others. CapCut has Apply to all and adjustment layers.
  • Then nudge each clip so skin tones match. They're what viewers notice most.

Camera tips

  • Pocket 4 D-Log looks grey and flat on purpose. Apply DJI's official LUT first (it converts the footage to normal colour), then adjust.
  • iPhone HDR video can look very different from other clips once posted. If iPhone and Pocket 4 clips don't match, try turning off Settings → Camera → Record Video → HDR Video for dance shoots.

Practise

  • Correct one clip in order: exposure → white balance → contrast → saturation.
  • Colour-match two clips from different cameras.
  • Apply DJI's LUT to a D-Log clip.
  • Film a test with iPhone HDR Video off and compare.
Light is easier to fix while filming than afterwards. Use one consistent light source (window light is beautiful), don't mix daylight with yellow bulbs, and lock exposure with a long-press on the iPhone.
